African dangerous game regulations typically mandate a minimum of .375 caliber (9.5mm) for Cape buffalo, elephant, and hippo. Many countries and professional hunters (PHs) prefer .40 caliber and above for thick-skinned dangerous game. These cartridges are selected for deep straight-line penetration with solids, reliable expansion with softs, and the authority to stop a charge at close range. Recoil management is not optional — you must be able to shoot these cartridges accurately under the most stressful conditions in hunting.

### [.375 H&H Magnum](/calibers/375-hh-magnum)

The .375 H&H is the most important cartridge in African hunting history and the legal minimum for dangerous game in most countries. A 300-grain Swift A-Frame at 2,530 fps penetrates deep enough for frontal brain shots on Cape buffalo and generates roughly 35 foot-pounds of recoil in a 9.5-pound rifle — manageable for experienced shooters. The .375 doubles as an outstanding plains game cartridge for eland, kudu, and zebra. Many PHs consider it the single best all-around African caliber. If you're making one trip and hunting both plains and dangerous game, the .375 H&H does both.

### [.416 Rigby](/calibers/416-rigby)

The .416 Rigby is the gentleman's dangerous game cartridge. Designed in 1911 for use in magnum-length Mauser actions, it pushes a 400-grain bullet at 2,400 fps with considerably more authority than the .375 H&H. The large case operates at moderate pressures (roughly 47,000 psi), which means reliable extraction in tropical heat where lesser cartridges can stick. Recoil is severe — 55+ foot-pounds — but the Rigby's smooth feeding and controlled-round-feed actions make it the choice of PHs who need a stopping rifle. The gold standard for Cape buffalo.

### [.416 Remington Magnum](/calibers/416-remington-magnum)

The .416 Rem Mag delivers .416 Rigby ballistics from a standard-length action. A 400-grain bullet at 2,400 fps in a lighter, shorter rifle. The trade-off is higher chamber pressure (62,000 psi) and sharper perceived recoil due to the lighter rifle weight. For hunters who want .416 performance without the expense and bulk of a magnum-length action, the .416 Rem Mag is the practical choice. Factory ammunition from Federal and Hornady is available with both solids and expanding bullets.

### [.458 Winchester Magnum](/calibers/458-winchester-magnum)

The .458 Win Mag was designed to replicate .450 Nitro Express performance from a bolt-action rifle. A 500-grain solid at 2,100-2,150 fps delivers massive momentum for straight-line penetration on elephant and hippo. Early ammunition had reliability issues in hot climates, but modern loads from Hornady, Federal, and Nosler are fully reliable. Recoil is punishing — 60+ foot-pounds — and practical only for shooters who train extensively. The .458 remains the standard stopping rifle cartridge for backup use by PHs across East and Southern Africa.

### [.460 Weatherby Magnum](/calibers/460-weatherby-magnum)

The .460 Weatherby is the most powerful commercially available shoulder-fired cartridge. A 500-grain bullet at 2,600 fps generates over 7,500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy and roughly 100 foot-pounds of free recoil. This is not a cartridge for casual use — it demands a muzzle brake, a stock designed for recoil management, and a shooter who has trained specifically with it. When everything must die right now — a charging elephant at 15 yards — the .460 Weatherby provides the maximum insurance available from a repeating rifle.

### [.378 Weatherby Magnum](/calibers/378-weatherby-magnum)

The .378 Weatherby pushes .375-caliber bullets 300-400 fps faster than the .375 H&H, generating roughly 6,000 foot-pounds of muzzle energy. A 300-grain TSX at 2,925 fps offers dramatically flatter trajectory for long-range shots on Cape buffalo in open terrain. Recoil is severe but manageable with a properly weighted rifle and muzzle brake. The .378 occupies the niche between the .375 H&H and the .416s — enough gun for anything in Africa with more reach than either.

### [.45-70 Government](/calibers/45-70-government)

The .45-70 is included not for African dangerous game but for North American dangerous game — specifically, brown bear defense in coastal Alaska. Modern lever-action loads from Buffalo Bore (405-grain hardcast at 2,000 fps from a Marlin 1895) generate massive close-range energy with straight-line penetration through heavy bone. Guide guns chambered in .45-70 are the standard backup rifle for Alaskan bear guides. Fast-handling, reliable in extreme cold, and devastating inside 100 yards.

### [.458 SOCOM](/calibers/458-socom)

The .458 SOCOM offers dangerous-game-class bullet diameter from the AR-15 platform. A 300-grain Barnes TSX at 1,900 fps from a 16-inch barrel provides adequate close-range performance for wild boar and potentially bear defense. It is not a substitute for the cartridges above on African dangerous game — velocity and energy are insufficient for thick-skinned animals. Included here as a modern alternative for North American big game in states that allow semi-automatic hunting.

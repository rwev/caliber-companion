Long-range precision is defined by numbers: ballistic coefficient, muzzle velocity, standard deviation, wind drift per MPH crosswind, and barrel life in round counts. Subjective preferences matter less here than in any other discipline — either the bullet arrives on target at 1,000 yards or it does not. These cartridges deliver the best combination of accuracy, shootability, barrel life, and cost-effectiveness for the precision shooter.

### [6.5 Creedmoor](/calibers/65-creedmoor)

The 6.5 Creedmoor dominates precision shooting for mechanical reasons, not marketing. The case geometry is optimized for consistent headspacing. The short action means a shorter, stiffer bolt throw and lighter rifle. Recoil of 11-12 foot-pounds in a 10-pound rifle lets the shooter spot impacts through the scope without losing sight picture. A 140-grain ELD-M at 2,710 fps stays supersonic past 1,400 yards and groups under 0.5 MOA from quality bolt actions. Barrel life runs 2,500-3,000 rounds. Factory match ammunition from Hornady and Federal is widely available and genuinely accurate. It does the most with the least.

### [.308 Winchester](/calibers/308-winchester)

The .308 built the precision rifle discipline and remains the best cartridge for learning long-range fundamentals. Barrel life exceeds 5,000 rounds, factory match ammo (Federal Gold Medal Match 175-grain SMK) costs less than any other precision cartridge, and brass is plentiful for handloaders. The .308 goes transonic around 1,000-1,050 yards with 175-grain match bullets, limiting its ceiling versus the 6.5 Creedmoor. But inside 800 yards — covering most practical precision shooting, LE applications, and designated marksman roles — it remains competitive. Start here if you are building your first precision rifle.

### [.300 PRC](/calibers/300-prc)

The .300 PRC was purpose-built to improve on the .300 Win Mag. Its longer case neck and sharper shoulder allow heavy, high-BC bullets like the 225-grain ELD-M (G1 BC .777) to seat optimally. That bullet at 2,810 fps stays supersonic past 1,700 yards and carries over 1,500 foot-pounds at 1,000. The U.S. military's adoption of the Mk22 ASR in .300 PRC validates the cartridge. Barrel life is 1,500-2,000 rounds, recoil is significant at 28-30 foot-pounds. This is not a volume training cartridge — it is a tool for extending precision engagement past 1,500 yards.

### [6mm Creedmoor](/calibers/6mm-creedmoor)

The 6mm Creedmoor necks the 6.5 CM case down to 6mm, pushing 105-108 grain match bullets at 3,000-3,050 fps. The higher velocity and excellent BCs produce roughly 15% less wind drift than the 6.5 CM at 800 yards with shorter time of flight. The tradeoff is barrel life: 2,000-2,500 rounds versus 2,500-3,000. For a PRS competitor shooting 3,000+ rounds per year, that means re-barreling more often. For a recreational long-range shooter at 500-1,000 rounds annually, barrel life is a non-issue. If you are chasing every competitive edge, the 6mm CM's lower recoil and flatter trajectory make it the top PRS choice.

### [.338 Lapua Magnum](/calibers/338-lapua-magnum)

The .338 Lapua redefined "long range." A 250-grain Sierra MatchKing at 2,950 fps stays supersonic past 1,800 yards and delivers over 1,000 foot-pounds at a mile. British CoH Craig Harrison's confirmed 2,707-yard kills in Afghanistan were made with it. For civilians, it is an indulgence: $4-8 per round for factory match, punishing recoil at 35+ foot-pounds, and 1,200-1,500 rounds of barrel life. But if your goal is accurate shooting at a mile and beyond, few cartridges have the factory ammo availability, brass quality, and proven track record of the .338 Lapua.

### [6.5 PRC](/calibers/65-prc)

The 6.5 PRC adds roughly 200 fps to the same high-BC bullets as the Creedmoor. A 143-grain ELD-X at 2,960 fps stays supersonic past 1,500 yards — pushing cleanly through the transonic zone where the standard Creedmoor begins to struggle. It requires a standard-length action and barrel life drops to 1,500-2,000 rounds. The 6.5 PRC occupies the sweet spot for shooters who want more range than the Creedmoor without stepping up to the recoil and cost of .300-caliber magnums.

### [.300 Winchester Magnum](/calibers/300-winchester-magnum)

The .300 Win Mag has been a military and competitive staple since the 1960s. A 215-grain Berger Hybrid at 2,850 fps stays supersonic past 1,500 yards. Factory match ammunition is more widely available than for the .300 PRC, brass selection is deeper, and decades of military and competitive use have produced an unmatched body of ballistic data and load development knowledge. Barrel life of 1,500-2,000 rounds and 25-28 foot-pounds of recoil are the costs. For the handloader, the combination of a .300 Win Mag and 215-grain Berger Hybrids remains one of the most competitive long-range setups you can build.

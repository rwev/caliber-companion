Bear defense is not hunting. You are stopping a 600-pound grizzly charging at 35 miles per hour from 30 yards — roughly 1.7 seconds to draw, aim, and fire. The bear may be coming head-on, skull angled downward, presenting the thickest bone and densest muscle as its first point of contact. Penetration is everything. Expanding bullets that work on deer are the wrong choice — hardcast lead flat-nose and heavy solids maintain their shape and drive straight through layers of fat, muscle, and bone to reach the vitals. Bear spray has a strong statistical record, but for those who carry a firearm in bear country, these calibers have proven themselves.

### [12 Gauge](/caliber-companion/calibers/12-gauge)

The 12-gauge with hardcast slugs is the most effective bear defense weapon available, and it is standard carry for many Alaska guides. A Brenneke Black Magic 602-grain slug at 1,475 fps delivers over 2,900 foot-pounds with a .729-inch frontal diameter. A pump-action loaded with five slugs gives you five chances to stop a charge with near-absolute mechanical reliability. The downsides are weight (7-8 pounds loaded), length, and the requirement to have it in your hands — not on your hip — when the encounter happens. Best used as a companion gun while someone else field-dresses game or breaks camp.

### [.44 Remington Magnum](/caliber-companion/calibers/44-magnum)

The .44 Magnum is the classic bear defense handgun caliber. A 305-grain hardcast flat-nose from Buffalo Bore at 1,325 fps from a 4-inch revolver generates roughly 1,190 foot-pounds and penetrates over 30 inches of gelatin in a straight line — enough to reach a grizzly's vitals from a frontal presentation. A Ruger Super Redhawk or S&W Model 629 with a 4-5 inch barrel fits a chest holster (Diamond D Custom or Gunfighters Inc Kenai) and is light enough to actually carry on an 8-mile hike. Practice with full-power hardcast loads, not just wadcutters.

### [10mm Auto](/caliber-companion/calibers/10mm-auto)

The 10mm's appeal is obvious: it is the most powerful cartridge available in a modern semi-auto. A Glock 20 holds 15 rounds, weighs 39 ounces loaded, and fires as fast as you pull the trigger. Buffalo Bore 220-grain hardcast at 1,200 fps generates 700-750 foot-pounds — meaningfully less than the .44 Magnum (roughly 60-65% of the energy) — but the 10mm compensates with capacity and speed. Fifteen rounds delivered rapidly gives you more opportunities for effective hits than six from a revolver. For black bear country, the 10mm is arguably ideal. In grizzly country, it is adequate with proper loads but represents the minimum serious recommendation.

### [.45-70 Government](/caliber-companion/calibers/45-70-government)

The .45-70 in a lever-action guide gun offers rifle-level power in a compact package. A Marlin 1895 with an 18.5-inch barrel loaded with Buffalo Bore 405-grain hardcast at 2,000 fps generates over 3,600 foot-pounds — substantially more than any handgun and competitive with slugs. The short lever gun is fast-handling with five rounds total. Several Alaska guides have switched from shotguns to .45-70 guide guns, citing better accuracy and deeper penetration. The platform requires two-handed operation and shouldering under stress, but for the hunter who keeps a rifle within arm's reach, it is brutally effective.

### [.454 Casull](/caliber-companion/calibers/454-casull)

The .454 Casull bridges the gap between the .44 Magnum and the extreme revolver cartridges. A 325-grain hardcast at 1,500 fps from a Ruger Super Redhawk generates over 1,600 foot-pounds — approaching rifle territory from a belt holster. The revolver also chambers .45 Colt for practice. The recoil is genuinely punishing, demanding serious grip strength and technique. If you train with it consistently and deliver accurate hits with full-power loads, the .454 is the most powerful practical bear defense handgun cartridge. If you flinch, you are better served by the .44 Magnum.

### [.357 Magnum](/caliber-companion/calibers/357-magnum)

The .357 Magnum is the minimum viable bear defense caliber. With 180-grain hardcast from Buffalo Bore at 1,400 fps from a 4-inch barrel, it penetrates over 24 inches of gelatin — comparable to the 10mm. For black bear defense, the .357 with hardcast loads is genuinely adequate. For grizzly defense, it is marginal — lacking the energy and bullet diameter to reliably penetrate a frontal skull. If you carry it in grizzly country because it is the most powerful handgun you can shoot well, load the heaviest hardcast available and understand you are accepting a compromise.

### [.375 H&H Magnum](/caliber-companion/calibers/375-hh-magnum)

The .375 H&H is the professional's bear defense rifle — carried by Alaska Peninsula guides facing the largest brown bears as routine. A 300-grain Nosler Partition at 2,530 fps generates over 4,200 foot-pounds, and the combination of heavy, large-diameter bullets and moderate velocity produces straight-line penetration through any tissue structure. It has been the minimum legal caliber for African dangerous game for nearly a century. In a CZ 550 or Winchester Model 70 Safari Express, the .375 H&H is the ultimate insurance policy. Recoil is stout at 35-40 foot-pounds. This is overkill for any scenario except the most dangerous — which is exactly when you want overkill.

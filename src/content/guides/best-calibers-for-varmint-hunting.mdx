Varmint hunting demands precision at distance, high volume of fire, and minimal pelt damage on predator-sized game. The ideal varmint cartridge shoots flat, bucks wind, and doesn't punish you after 200 rounds in a prairie dog town. Every cartridge here excels at different aspects of the varmint discipline.

### [.223 Remington](/caliber-companion/calibers/223-remington)

The .223 is the default varmint cartridge in America, and for good reason. A 55-grain V-MAX at 3,240 fps shoots flat to 300 yards, produces dramatic terminal effect on prairie dogs, and generates so little recoil (roughly 3.5 foot-pounds in an 8-pound rifle) that you can spot your own hits through the scope. Brass is dirt cheap, components are always in stock, and every rifle manufacturer makes a varmint-weight .223. For all-around varmint work from 50 to 400 yards, the .223 is the cartridge to beat.

### [.22-250 Remington](/caliber-companion/calibers/22-250-remington)

When the .223 runs out of steam, the .22-250 is still going. Pushing a 55-grain bullet past 3,680 fps, it shoots meaningfully flatter and hits harder at 400+ yards. The price is more powder, more barrel heat, and about twice the recoil of a .223 — still completely manageable. The .22-250 is the gold standard for long-range prairie dog shooting in wide-open western terrain where 300-yard shots are the norm, not the exception.

### [.204 Ruger](/caliber-companion/calibers/204-ruger)

The .204 Ruger pushes a 32-grain V-MAX at over 4,200 fps — the fastest commercially loaded cartridge under .30 caliber. The result is laser-flat trajectory and explosive terminal effect on anything smaller than a coyote. Recoil is imperceptible. The downside is wind sensitivity: at 32 grains, a 10-mph crosswind pushes the bullet nearly twice as far as a .22-250 at 400 yards. Best for calm-air shooting at prairie dogs and ground squirrels where speed matters more than wind resistance.

### [.22 LR](/caliber-companion/calibers/22-lr)

For close-range varmint control — barn rats, gophers, rabbits inside 75 yards — nothing matches the .22 LR for economy and convenience. CCI Stingers or Mini-Mags are quiet enough to shoot near livestock, cheap enough to shoot all day, and accurate enough in a bolt-action Ruger American Rimfire or CZ 457 to hit a squirrel-sized target at 50 yards reliably. The .22 LR has no business past 100 yards on varmints, but inside that range it's the most practical tool available.

### [.17 HMR](/caliber-companion/calibers/17-hmr)

The .17 HMR splits the difference between .22 LR economy and centerfire performance. A 17-grain V-MAX at 2,550 fps shoots far flatter than .22 LR and produces explosive fragmentation on prairie dogs at 150 yards. It's loud enough to spook nearby game but quiet enough for semi-rural pest control. Ammunition costs roughly $0.30-0.50 per round — about 5× the cost of .22 LR but half the cost of .223. Excellent for dedicated small varmint rifles.

### [.243 Winchester](/caliber-companion/calibers/243-winchester)

The .243 is a varmint cartridge that can double as a deer rifle. With 58-grain V-MAX loads at 3,750 fps, it's devastating on coyotes and groundhogs at 400+ yards. Then swap to 100-grain Partitions and you're hunting whitetail. This versatility makes the .243 the best choice for hunters who want one rifle for predator calling season and deer season. The trade-off is barrel life — high-velocity 6mm rounds eat barrels faster than .223.

### [.224 Valkyrie](/caliber-companion/calibers/224-valkyrie)

Federal designed the .224 Valkyrie specifically for long-range performance from the AR-15 platform. An 88-grain ELD Match at 2,675 fps stays supersonic past 1,300 yards and bucks wind far better than the .223. For AR-platform varmint shooters who want to reach past 500 yards, the Valkyrie offers genuine capability. Ammunition selection is limited compared to .223 but improving.

### [5.56×45mm NATO](/caliber-companion/calibers/556x45mm-nato)

The 5.56 NATO is functionally identical to .223 Remington in most varmint applications, with the caveat that 5.56 chambers handle slightly higher pressures. Military surplus M193 (55-grain FMJ) is among the cheapest centerfire ammunition available and fragments effectively on small varmints. For high-volume prairie dog shooting where cost per round matters more than terminal precision, surplus 5.56 through an AR-15 is hard to beat.

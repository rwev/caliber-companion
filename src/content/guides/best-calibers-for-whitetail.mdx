Any cartridge on this list will cleanly kill a whitetail at reasonable distances with proper bullet placement — a 150-pound deer is not a hard animal to put down. What separates these recommendations is how forgiving they are of imperfect shot placement, how versatile they are across terrain and hunting styles, and how accessible they are to the broadest range of hunters.

### [.308 Winchester](/caliber-companion/calibers/308-winchester)

The .308 does nothing poorly and most things exceptionally well. A 150-grain Nosler Partition at 2,820 fps delivers more than enough energy at any ethical shooting distance, and moderate recoil (15-17 foot-pounds in a 7.5-pound rifle) means most hunters shoot it without developing a flinch. Ammunition is available everywhere — from rural gas stations to big-box retailers — in bullet weights from 125 to 180 grains. If you own one centerfire rifle for all your deer hunting, make it a .308.

### [.270 Winchester](/caliber-companion/calibers/270-winchester)

Jack O'Connor was right: the .270 is a near-perfect deer cartridge. The classic 130-grain load at 3,060 fps shoots flatter than the .308, carries over 1,500 foot-pounds past 400 yards, and drops only about 7 inches at 300 with a 200-yard zero. For open-country hunters in western Kansas, the Dakotas, or southern Alberta, that flat trajectory provides a genuine practical advantage.

### [.30-06 Springfield](/caliber-companion/calibers/30-06-springfield)

The .30-06 has killed more North American deer than any cartridge in history. It offers slightly more velocity than the .308 — enough to matter at longer ranges but not enough to dramatically change recoil. If you inherited your grandfather's .30-06, you do not need something newer. Modern premium bullets like the Hornady ELD-X and Barnes TTSX have only widened the gap between what the .30-06 can do and what deer hunting actually demands.

### [6.5 Creedmoor](/caliber-companion/calibers/65-creedmoor)

A 143-grain ELD-X at 2,700 fps generates less recoil than a .308 but carries comparable energy at 400 yards with significantly less wind drift. The high-BC 6.5mm bullets cut through air more efficiently, making the Creedmoor more forgiving of range estimation errors. For hunters shooting from stands across open terrain at known distances, this may be the best option on the list. Load 129-grain Hornady Interlock for budget hunts or 143-grain ELD-X for the best terminal performance available.

### [.243 Winchester](/caliber-companion/calibers/243-winchester)

The .243 is the best beginner and youth deer cartridge ever made. At roughly 10-11 foot-pounds of recoil — about 60% of a .308 — a young hunter who puts a 100-grain Nosler Partition through both lungs will recover a deer far more reliably than one who flinches a .30-06 into a gut shot. The .243 carries over 1,000 foot-pounds past 300 yards with quality bullets. Use 95-grain Hornady SST or 100-grain Federal Fusion and keep shots inside 300 yards.

### [.30-30 Winchester](/caliber-companion/calibers/30-30-winchester)

The .30-30 in a lever-action rifle has killed more whitetail than any other cartridge-platform combination in American history. The Marlin 336 and Winchester 94 are lightweight, fast-handling, and perfectly suited to the close-range timber hunting that defines whitetail season across the eastern U.S. Inside 150 yards, the .30-30 hits with authority. Hornady LEVERevolution 160-grain FTX extends the effective range by 50-75 yards with improved ballistic coefficient. If your shots rarely exceed 100 yards, the lever gun is still the best tool in the safe.

### [.350 Legend](/caliber-companion/calibers/350-legend)

The .350 Legend exists for straight-wall cartridge regulations. Ohio, Iowa, Indiana, and other Midwestern states restrict deer hunting to straight-wall cartridges in their shotgun zones, and the .350 Legend is Winchester's answer. It generates roughly .30-30 energy from an AR-15 or lightweight bolt action with straighter trajectory and softer recoil. A 180-grain Power-Point at 2,100 fps remains effective to 200 yards. If you hunt in a straight-wall-only state, this is the best option available.

### [7mm-08 Remington](/caliber-companion/calibers/7mm-08-remington)

The 7mm-08 is the most underrated deer cartridge in America. It necks the .308 case down to 7mm, offering better ballistic coefficients at comparable weights with roughly 13-14 foot-pounds of recoil. It shoots flatter and drifts less than the .308. The 7mm-08 is everything the .243 is but with 40% more bullet weight and substantially better terminal performance. If you want a cartridge a 12-year-old can start with and a seasoned hunter never outgrows, this is it.

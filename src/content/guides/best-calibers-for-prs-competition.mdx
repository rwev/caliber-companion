Precision Rifle Series (PRS) and NRL competition demands consistent hits on small steel targets from 300 to 1,200+ yards in varying wind conditions, often under time pressure. The ideal PRS cartridge minimizes wind drift, maintains supersonic flight past 1,200 yards, generates manageable recoil for fast follow-up shots, and offers acceptable barrel life for a season of practice and matches.

### [6mm Creedmoor](/calibers/6mm-creedmoor)

The 6mm Creedmoor dominates PRS leaderboards. A 108-115 grain Berger or ELD-M at 2,950 fps offers the best wind performance of any 6mm cartridge in a short-action platform. Wind drift at 1,000 yards is roughly 15-20% less than 6.5 Creedmoor with comparable bullet designs. Recoil is about 30% less than 6.5 Creedmoor, allowing faster transitions and better visual tracking of impacts. The trade-off is barrel life: expect 2,000-2,500 rounds before accuracy degrades, meaning competitive shooters may go through 2-3 barrels per season. If winning matters more than barrel cost, the 6mm Creedmoor is the consensus choice.

### [6.5 Creedmoor](/calibers/65-creedmoor)

The 6.5 Creedmoor remains the most practical PRS cartridge. A 140-grain ELD-M at 2,710 fps stays supersonic past 1,400 yards, drifts less than .308 by 30-40% at 1,000 yards, and offers 3,000-4,000 round barrel life — significantly more than any 6mm option. Factory match ammunition from Hornady, Federal, and others is widely available and genuinely competition-grade. For shooters who practice heavily and don't want to rebarrel every season, the 6.5 Creedmoor is the best balance of performance and longevity.

### [6.5×47 Lapua](/calibers/65x47-lapua)

The 6.5×47 Lapua is the handloader's precision cartridge. Lapua brass is the finest in the world — consistent weight, uniform flash holes, and it lasts 15-20 firings with proper neck sizing. Ballistically it sits between 6.5 Creedmoor and 6.5 PRC, pushing a 140-grain Berger at 2,750 fps with excellent accuracy and 3,500+ round barrel life. The downside is zero factory match ammunition — this is a reloader-only cartridge. For PRS shooters who handload every round anyway, the 6.5×47 offers a genuine edge in brass quality and consistency.

### [.308 Winchester](/calibers/308-winchester)

The .308 is the default cartridge for PRS Tactical/Production divisions, where equipment restrictions level the playing field. A 175-grain Sierra MatchKing at 2,600 fps is effective to 1,000 yards but requires significantly more wind holds than the 6mm and 6.5mm cartridges. In open division PRS, the .308 is not competitive — it simply drifts too much past 800 yards. But for beginners, for gas gun divisions, and for shooters who value the challenge of reading wind with a cartridge that punishes mistakes, the .308 teaches precision rifle fundamentals better than anything else.

### [6.5 PRC](/calibers/65-prc)

The 6.5 PRC pushes 147-grain ELD-M bullets at 2,900+ fps — roughly 200 fps faster than 6.5 Creedmoor with the same bullet. The result is extended supersonic range past 1,600 yards and meaningfully less wind drift beyond 1,000. The cost is sharper recoil, shorter barrel life (2,500-3,000 rounds), and slightly more muzzle blast. In ELR (Extreme Long Range) competitions where targets extend past 1,500 yards, the 6.5 PRC has a legitimate advantage over the Creedmoor. For standard PRS distances (300-1,200 yards), the added recoil may cost more points than the ballistic advantage gains.

### [.300 Norma Magnum](/calibers/300-norma-magnum)

The .300 Norma is the dominant ELR cartridge, adopted by SOCOM for the Advanced Sniper Rifle program. A 230-grain Berger Hybrid at 2,900 fps stays supersonic past 2,000 yards with enormous energy retention. In dedicated ELR matches with targets at 1,500-2,500 yards, nothing short of .375 or .408 outperforms it. Recoil is severe and barrel life is 1,200-1,500 rounds. This is a purpose-built competition cartridge for extreme distances, not a general PRS choice.

### [.338 Lapua Magnum](/calibers/338-lapua-magnum)

The .338 Lapua is the original ELR competition cartridge and remains viable for matches featuring targets beyond 2,000 yards. A 300-grain Berger at 2,700 fps carries over 1,000 foot-pounds past a mile. Recoil is brutal — 35+ foot-pounds in a 15-pound rifle — and barrel life is roughly 1,500 rounds. The .300 Norma has largely supplanted it in competition due to better bullet selection and slightly less recoil, but the .338 Lapua remains the standard for military-style long-range engagements and King of 2 Mile competitions.

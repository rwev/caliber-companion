## The Power Factor Equation

Competitive pistol shooting revolves around one key concept: **power factor**. Power factor (PF) is calculated as bullet weight (grains) × velocity (fps) ÷ 1000. In USPSA, you need a minimum of 125 PF for Minor and 165 PF for Major scoring. In IDPA, minimum PF varies by division but is generally 125.

This single number drives nearly every caliber decision in competition. The question isn't just "what's the best caliber?" — it's "what makes power factor with the least recoil?"

## 9mm Luger — The Dominant Choice

**Best for:** USPSA Production, Carry Optics, IDPA, Steel Challenge

9mm Luger dominates competitive pistol shooting, and it's not even close. In USPSA Production and Carry Optics divisions — where the vast majority of competitors shoot — 9mm is the overwhelming choice. Here's why:

- **Lowest recoil that makes Minor PF.** A 124gr bullet at ~1,020 fps makes 126 PF with minimal felt recoil. A 147gr bullet at ~860 fps makes the same PF even softer.
- **Highest capacity.** In divisions with magazine capacity limits (17+ rounds), 9mm fits the most rounds.
- **Cheapest to shoot.** At $0.18–0.75/round, 9mm lets you practice more for the same budget.
- **Best ammunition variety.** More competition-specific loads exist in 9mm than any other caliber.

The typical competition 9mm load is a 147gr bullet at 860-880 fps (PF ~127) or a 124gr bullet at 1,020-1,040 fps (PF ~127). Both make Minor with comfort margin and excellent accuracy.

**Recommended loads:** 147gr Federal Syntech, 147gr Winchester Active Duty, handloaded 147gr RN at ~860 fps.

## .40 S&W — The Major Power Factor Play

**Best for:** USPSA Limited, Limited-10

In USPSA Limited division, Major power factor (165 PF) provides a significant scoring advantage — hits in the C and D zones score more points. This makes .40 S&W the caliber of choice for Limited shooters:

- **Makes Major PF efficiently.** A 180gr bullet at ~920 fps hits 165 PF with manageable recoil.
- **Double-stack capacity.** Unlike .45 ACP, .40 S&W fits in wide-body 2011 frames with 20+ round capacity.
- **The math works.** Major scoring advantage × high capacity = competitive edge.

The .40 S&W exists in modern competition primarily because of USPSA's Major/Minor scoring system. If you're not shooting Limited or Open division for Major PF, there's little reason to choose .40 over 9mm.

**Recommended loads:** Handloaded 180gr at 920 fps, or 180gr at minimum PF for softest recoil.

## .45 ACP — Single-Stack and Nostalgia

**Best for:** USPSA Single Stack, IDPA Custom Defensive Pistol

.45 ACP's role in modern competition is primarily in divisions that require or benefit from the larger caliber:

- **USPSA Single Stack:** Makes Major PF easily with 230gr loads at 720+ fps.
- **IDPA CDP:** Requires .45 ACP minimum caliber.

A 200gr SWC at 835 fps (167 PF) is a popular competition load that balances recoil with reliable Major PF. The downside is always capacity — single-stack .45 magazines hold 8-10 rounds versus 17+ in 9mm.

## .38 Special — Revolver Division

**Best for:** USPSA Revolver, IDPA Revolver, Steel Challenge (Rimfire not eligible)

For revolver competition, .38 Special loaded to Minor PF is the standard. Light 148gr wadcutter loads at ~750 fps (111 PF) offer minimum recoil, though you'll need 125+ PF for USPSA.

Wheelgun competitors typically use moonclip-compatible revolvers (S&W 686, 929) with .38 Special wadcutter loads tuned to just over minimum PF.

## Steel Challenge Specifics

Steel Challenge is pure speed — no power factor advantages for Major. This makes 9mm Minor the universal choice. The lightest, softest-shooting 9mm load you can reliably cycle your gun with is the correct answer. Many top Steel Challenge shooters use 125gr or 147gr loads at absolute minimum power factor.

## The Bottom Line

| Division | Best Caliber | Why |
|---|---|---|
| USPSA Production | 9mm | Lowest recoil at Minor PF |
| USPSA Carry Optics | 9mm | Same — speed wins |
| USPSA Limited | .40 S&W | Major PF in double-stack |
| USPSA Open | 9mm Major (9mm +P+) | High capacity, compensated |
| USPSA Single Stack | .45 ACP | Required for Major |
| IDPA SSP/CCP | 9mm | Lowest recoil, highest capacity |
| IDPA CDP | .45 ACP | Division requirement |
| Steel Challenge | 9mm | Pure speed, no PF advantage |

For 80%+ of competition pistol shooters, 9mm is the answer. The remaining 20% are shooting .40 S&W for Major PF in Limited division or .45 ACP in single-stack/CDP divisions.

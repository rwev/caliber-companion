A suppressor reduces sound by 25-35 decibels, but true hearing-safe shooting (below 140 dB) requires subsonic ammunition — bullets traveling below roughly 1,100 fps at the muzzle. The best suppressor cartridges are those designed from the ground up for subsonic use or whose standard loads already operate near the sound barrier.

### [.300 Blackout](/caliber-companion/calibers/300-blackout)

The .300 Blackout was designed specifically for suppressed use in the AR-15 platform. Its 220-grain subsonic loads at 1,010 fps are hearing-safe through a quality suppressor, cycle standard AR gas systems reliably, and deliver more terminal energy than any other subsonic AR cartridge. The same rifle, same magazines, same bolt — just swap uppers and you go from supersonic 110-grain hunting loads to whisper-quiet 220-grain subsonics. No other cartridge matches this versatility. If you're buying one suppressor host, make it a .300 Blackout.

### [9mm Luger](/caliber-companion/calibers/9mm-luger)

The 9mm is the most practical suppressed handgun cartridge. Standard 147-grain loads at 950-1,000 fps are already subsonic, and through a good suppressor like a Dead Air Wolfman, they produce roughly 130-135 dB — genuinely hearing-safe. The combination of a suppressed Glock 19 or SIG P226 with 147-grain Federal HST is the standard-issue setup for agencies that run suppressed sidearms. Ammunition availability and cost make the 9mm the easiest suppressed caliber to feed.

### [.45 ACP](/caliber-companion/calibers/45-acp)

The .45 ACP is inherently subsonic — standard 230-grain loads run 830-850 fps, well below the sound barrier. This makes every .45 ACP load suppressor-ready without special subsonic ammunition. Through a suppressor, the .45 is remarkably quiet, and the heavy 230-grain bullet delivers substantial energy despite low velocity. The classic suppressed platform is a 1911 or FN FNX-45 Tactical with a threaded barrel. The drawback is reduced magazine capacity compared to 9mm.

### [.22 LR](/caliber-companion/calibers/22-lr)

Suppressed .22 LR is as quiet as shooting gets with a firearm. CCI Quiet (40-grain at 710 fps) through a Dead Air Mask sounds like a staple gun. Standard-velocity .22 LR (40-grain at 1,070 fps) is still subsonic and nearly as quiet. A suppressed 10/22 or Ruger Mark IV is the ultimate backyard pest control tool and the most fun you can have introducing new shooters to firearms. The only caliber where suppressed shooting approaches Hollywood-quiet levels.

### [5.56×45mm NATO](/caliber-companion/calibers/556x45mm-nato)

The 5.56 is not ideal for subsonic use — its light bullets lose effectiveness rapidly below supersonic speeds. But suppressed supersonic 5.56 is still significantly quieter than unsuppressed, reducing sound by 30+ dB and virtually eliminating muzzle flash. For military and law enforcement applications where terminal performance matters more than absolute sound reduction, suppressed supersonic 5.56 through an MK18 or similar SBR is the standard. Expect 135-140 dB — not hearing-safe, but dramatically better than 165+ dB unsuppressed.

### [.308 Winchester](/caliber-companion/calibers/308-winchester)

Suppressed .308 serves the precision rifle community. A 175-grain Sierra MatchKing through a Thunder Beast Ultra or Dead Air Sandman-S reduces felt recoil noticeably, eliminates muzzle flash, and cuts sound to roughly 136-140 dB. Subsonic .308 loads (200+ grain at 1,050 fps) exist but sacrifice the cartridge's primary advantage — long-range ballistics. Most .308 suppressor users run standard supersonic loads and accept the sonic crack, gaining recoil reduction and flash suppression rather than Hollywood-quiet operation.

### [4.6×30mm](/caliber-companion/calibers/46x30mm)

Designed for the HK MP7 PDW, the 4.6×30mm was built for suppressed close-quarters use by special operations units. Its small, high-velocity projectile is lethal against soft body armor at close range while generating minimal recoil and blast. Subsonic loads are available for maximum sound suppression. Civilian availability is extremely limited — this is included for completeness as a purpose-built suppressor cartridge.

### [.458 SOCOM](/caliber-companion/calibers/458-socom)

The .458 SOCOM fires a massive 300-600 grain bullet from the AR-15 platform. Subsonic 600-grain loads at 900 fps are extraordinarily quiet through a suppressor while delivering devastating terminal effect at close range. Think of it as a suppressed sledgehammer. Practical applications include suppressed hog hunting and home defense where maximum stopping power in a subsonic package is desired. Ammunition is expensive and barrel life is limited, but nothing else combines this much mass with this little noise.
